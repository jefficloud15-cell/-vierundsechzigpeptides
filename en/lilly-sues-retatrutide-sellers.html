Lilly sues six companies for selling retatrutide labelled 'research use only' for human use

The drugmaker says it has reported more than 200 people and entities and over 14,000 listings. The case tests what the RUO label really means.

Vierundsechzig Newsroom · Published Nov 21, 2026Share: WhatsApp

On August 12, 2026, Eli Lilly announced six lawsuits against companies that, according to the company, sell unapproved versions of retatrutide, its triple GIP, GLP-1 and glucagon receptor agonist that is still in clinical trials.

What Lilly alleges

The defendants are Aesthetic Envy Cosmetic Centers, Astra, Legendary Peptides, Striker Pharmacy, Texas Peptides and Lone Star Peptide. The central allegation, as reported by Healio, is that these companies falsely claim their products are "research use only" when they are actually intended for people.

Beyond the lawsuits, Lilly said it has reported more than 200 people and entities to FDA, the Department of Justice, state attorneys general and professional boards, and more than 14,000 websites, ads, posts and listings to platforms and internet providers. Its chief medical officer, David Hyman, described the black-market product as unverified, unapproved and not worth the risk.

The context: FDA warnings

In June 2026, FDA had already warned about retatrutide and cagrilintide: neither is approved for any condition or can be used in compounding. The agency sent letters to telehealth companies, ingredient distributors and repackaging facilities, and noted that some products were sold for human use with dosing instructions despite carrying a "research" label.

Lilly, for its part, plans to file for US approval of retatrutide in the first quarter of 2027.

What it means for the RUO market

The case carries an important lesson: the RUO label is not a formality. A product labelled "research use only" that is promoted with human-use instructions, personal testimonials or promised results is, in practice, no longer a research material.
