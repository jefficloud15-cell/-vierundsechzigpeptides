How to read a certificate of analysis

The document that separates a serious supplier from an improvised one — what each number means and what should make you suspicious.

Technical guide · Vierundsechzig Peptides CRUpdated: Nov 11, 2026

The fields that matter

FieldWhat it tells you
Name & lot numberWhich batch it refers to — must match the vial label.
Test dateWhen the analysis was done.
MethodUsually HPLC for purity and MS for identity.
Purity (%)Share of the content that is the declared compound.
Molecular weightTheoretical vs observed — they should match closely.
Lab & signatureWho ran the analysis and who validates it.

Red flags

  • No lot number, or a lot that doesn't match the vial.
  • No lab name.
  • Just a percentage — no chromatogram or method.
  • The same certificate reused for different products or dates.

FAQ

What is a certificate of analysis (CoA)?

The lab report documenting test results for one specific lot: purity, identity, appearance and method.

What does ≥99% HPLC purity mean?

In the chromatogram, the compound's peak accounts for at least 99% of the total detected peak area; the rest are impurities or synthesis fragments.

Does HPLC confirm the identity?

Not on its own. Identity is confirmed by mass spectrometry (MS), comparing observed and theoretical molecular weight.
