What is a peptide?

A peptide is a molecule made of amino acids linked in a chain by peptide bonds. Amino acids are the same building blocks that make proteins; the difference is size. By convention, a short chain, usually fewer than 50 amino acids, is called a peptide, and a longer chain that folds into a complex three-dimensional structure is called a protein.

Why are they studied so much?

Many peptides act as signalling molecules: they bind a specific receptor and switch a process on or off. That precision makes them highly interesting for research. A 2021 review in Nature Reviews Drug Discovery counted more than 80 approved peptide drugs worldwide and more than 150 in clinical development (Muttenthaler et al., 2021). Each of them started with years of lab work on research-grade compounds.

How are they made?

Almost all research peptides are produced by solid-phase peptide synthesis (SPPS), introduced by Bruce Merrifield in 1963: the chain is built one amino acid at a time on a solid resin, so leftover reagents can be washed away at each step. It earned him the 1984 Nobel Prize in Chemistry and is the basis of automated synthesis today.

  1. Synthesis: the exact amino acid sequence is assembled.
  2. Purification: the correct peptide is separated from incomplete chains by preparative HPLC.
  3. Lyophilization: it is frozen and the water removed under vacuum, leaving a dry powder or cake that is far more stable than a solution. Step by step in what is a lyophilized peptide.
  4. Batch testing: a laboratory measures purity and identity and issues the certificate.

What does "research use only" mean?

A research peptide (RUO) is supplied for laboratory studies: assays in cells, tissues or experimental models. It is not a drug, is not approved for human or veterinary use, and is not sold with usage directions.

How is quality verified?

  • How much of the content is the right peptide? Measured by HPLC (high-performance liquid chromatography). The result is the purity, for example ≥ 99%.
  • Is it really that peptide? Confirmed by mass spectrometry, which compares the measured molecular weight with the theoretical one.

Both results appear on the certificate of analysis (CoA), which must match the batch number on the vial. Step by step: how to read a certificate of analysis.

How are they stored?

Lyophilized, a peptide tolerates time far better than in solution. It is generally kept cold, dry and away from light; once reconstituted, it is refrigerated and keeps for a shorter time. More on the diluent in bacteriostatic water.

Frequently asked questions

What is a research peptide?

A peptide made by chemical synthesis and supplied as laboratory material (RUO) for studies in cells, tissues or experimental models. It is not a drug and is not intended for human use.

What is the difference between a peptide and a protein?

Size and structure. A peptide is a short chain of amino acids, usually fewer than 50; a protein is a longer chain that folds into a complex three-dimensional structure.

Why do peptides come as a powder?

Because they are lyophilized: frozen and dried under vacuum. In that state they are far more stable than in solution and handle storage and shipping better.

How do I know a peptide is good quality?

From its batch certificate of analysis: HPLC gives the purity and mass spectrometry confirms the identity. The batch on the certificate must match the vial label.
