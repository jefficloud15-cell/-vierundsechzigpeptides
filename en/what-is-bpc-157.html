What BPC-157 is and what the published research says

BPC-157 is a synthetic 15-amino-acid peptide derived from a protein in human gastric juice. Here is what peer-reviewed publications say about its structure, the mechanisms studied, and the evidence that exists — and does not — in humans.

In short
  • BPC-157 is a synthetic 15-amino-acid peptide (a pentadecapeptide) corresponding to a partial sequence of a protein described in human gastric juice, the body protection compound.
  • Its published sequence is Gly-Glu-Pro-Pro-Pro-Gly-Lys-Pro-Ala-Asp-Asp-Ala-Gly-Leu-Val and its molecular mass is 1419 Da.
  • Almost all published research is preclinical: cell cultures and animal models. A 2025 systematic review included 36 studies, 35 of them preclinical.
  • It is not approved as a medicine. In October 2026 it was included in a US interim compounding policy, which is not an approval.

Where the sequence comes from

BPC-157 was not discovered as a whole molecule but as a fragment. In the 1990s, Predrag Sikirić's group described a protein in human gastric juice that they called body protection compound, and isolated from it a 15-amino-acid partial sequence that retained activity in experimental models. That sequence is what is synthesized in the lab today and known as BPC-157.

The 15 amino acids of the published sequence, in order. The three consecutive prolines (positions 3 to 5) are one reason it is described as stable against acid digestion.

It is a short peptide: for scale, retatrutide and tirzepatide have 39 amino acids and human insulin has 51. That size difference matters, because small molecules are easier to synthesize and also degrade differently.

Which mechanisms have been studied

The preclinical literature points to several pathways. These are the ones that appear most often in peer-reviewed studies:

Pathway studiedWhat was observed, and in which model
Angiogenesis (VEGFR2)In chick chorioallantoic membrane, human endothelial cells and ischemic rat hind-limb muscle, higher vessel density and increased VEGFR2 expression were reported, with activation of the VEGFR2-Akt-eNOS pathway (Hsieh et al., 2017).
Fibroblast migration (FAK and paxillin)In rat Achilles tendon fibroblasts, greater outgrowth from the explant, more migration and phosphorylation of FAK and paxillin were reported, with no direct change in proliferation (Chang et al., 2011).
Growth hormone receptorIn the same fibroblasts, a microarray analysis found the growth hormone receptor among the most up-regulated genes (Chang et al., 2014).
Nitric oxide and inflammationReviews describe interaction with the nitric oxide and dopamine systems and reduced inflammatory cytokines in animal models (Sikirić, 1999; Vasireddi et al., 2025).

Important: all of the above is mechanism in cells and animals. A mechanism described in culture does not by itself predict what happens in a whole organism.

What is published in humans

Very little, and that is worth stating plainly. A systematic review published in HSS Journal in 2025 searched PubMed, Cochrane and Embase from database inception to June 2024. Of 544 articles identified, it included 36 studies: 35 preclinical and 1 clinical.

A narrative review published in Current Reviews in Musculoskeletal Medicine in December 2025 reached a similar count: only three pilot studies in humans, on intra-articular knee pain, interstitial cystitis and intravenous safety. No adverse effects were reported, but the authors stress that rigorous, large-scale trials are lacking.

In other words: the human database amounts to a few dozen people, with no large randomized trials. This is exactly the kind of compound the research-use-only category exists for.

Where it stands with regulators

BPC-157 is not approved as a medicine in any country. In the United States the needle did move in 2026: in October, the Department of Health and Human Services published an interim enforcement discretion policy that includes BPC-157 among six peptides that 503A pharmacies may compound under certain conditions. That policy is not an approval and does not establish safety or effectiveness; we covered it in detail in this Journal piece.

In professional sport its use is banned. And in Costa Rica, as in most countries, there is no health registration for this compound: what is legally sold is laboratory material.

What this means for research

For a laboratory, BPC-157 is a compound with a broad preclinical base and a minimal clinical one. That makes it interesting as an object of study and, at the same time, demands care with language: any claim about effects in people runs ahead of what the literature currently supports.

In practice, two things matter more than the rest when working with it: that the material is what it says it is, with a certificate of analysis backing the identity and purity of the batch, and that it is stored properly, because it is a lyophilized peptide and temperature and light determine how long it lasts.

Frequently asked questions

What exactly is BPC-157?

It is a synthetic 15-amino-acid peptide whose sequence corresponds to a partial fragment of a protein described in human gastric juice, called body protection compound. Its molecular mass is 1419 Da.

Is BPC-157 approved as a medicine?

No. It has no approval as a medicine in any country. In October 2026 the US HHS included it in an interim policy for compounding at 503A pharmacies, which is not an approval and does not establish safety or effectiveness.

How much human research exists?

Very little. A 2025 systematic review included 36 studies, of which only 1 was clinical, and a narrative review from the same year identified just three human pilot studies. No large randomized trials have been published.

Are BPC-157 and TB-500 the same thing?

No. They are two different peptides, with different sequences and origins. They appear together in tissue-repair literature and share a place in blends such as KLOW and GLOW, but they are not interchangeable.

Why is it described as stable?

Publications describe it as resistant to acid digestion in the stomach, and its structure with three consecutive prolines is cited as part of that stability. In the lab it is handled lyophilized, which is its stable storage form.
