1 mg = 1,000 mcg · A 1 ml U-100 syringe = 100 units (each unit = 0.01 ml).

How the math works

  1. Concentration = (vial mg × 1,000) ÷ ml of diluent, in mcg per ml.
  2. Volume per aliquot = target mcg ÷ concentration, in ml.
  3. U-100 units = volume in ml × 100.

Worked example

A 10 mg vial reconstituted with 2 ml of bacteriostatic water gives 5,000 mcg/ml. A 250 mcg aliquot is 0.05 ml — 5 units on a U-100 syringe — and the vial yields 40 equal aliquots.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing up mg and mcg — a factor-of-1,000 error. Always check the vial unit.
  • Using a non U-100 syringe — U-40 or other scales change the unit conversion.
  • Not labeling the vial — write the date and concentration once reconstituted.
